# Prompts das imagens editoriais

Ferramenta: `image_gen` integrada. Saídas finais: `public/editorial/`, listadas em [CREDITOS.md](CREDITOS.md). As três imagens complementares usam a primeira apenas como referência de local, luz e tratamento.

## Principal

Use case: photorealistic-natural. Produce an original high-end editorial campaign photograph for a software company's institutional website, showing the working context of suppliers to construction, NOT a construction company's advertisement. Landscape 16:9, wide shot. Contemporary but believable materials showroom, an architect/specification professional and a commercial advisor quietly reviewing physical stone and wood finish samples on a broad worktable. TWO adult people, natural candid expressions, not looking at camera, no handshake. Place both people and the material-selection activity entirely in the RIGHT HALF, approximately 62–88% of the image width. Leave the LEFT 45% visually calm: the deep petrol-toned shaded wall of the same showroom and a subtle lit shelf/architectural texture, with absolutely NO people there, suitable for a white headline later in HTML. The left is connected to the scene, not a blank studio backdrop. The photo should have a clear direction of warm daylight entering from the far right, dimensional lateral shadows, natural skin, mineral stone, honey-toned wood, warm off-white surfaces, deep petrol architectural accents. Elegant contemporary campaign photography, tactile material detail, subtle 35mm grain, refined natural grading, no heavy green filter. Frame the subjects from waist up, their simple natural hand gestures legible over the table. Keep the upper left dark and uncluttered for an official logo inserted later by code. Maintain enough room above and below for responsive crops. No visible monitor, phone, laptop, software, charts, financial data, words or logos; no brand markings on clothing or building, no construction site, hard hats, floating objects, 3D objects, arches or portals, no watermark. This is a generic illustrative editorial scene, not an actual ARCCO team or client.
